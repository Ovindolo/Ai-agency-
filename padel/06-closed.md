# Padel — Closed

**Decision:** the client declined the padel direction. Agency recommended it; client said no. That is the client's call and it is final.

**Cost of the decision: €0.** No company registered, no supplier contacted, no stock ordered, no ads run, no domain bought. The entire exercise stayed on the correct side of the first spend.

---

## What the agency got wrong

Not the analysis. The market data holds: 19.4M players, Europe holds two-thirds of ~58,300 courts, the overgrip is a genuine consumable, and the Romanian tax regime is favourable. None of that was wrong.

**The selection criteria were wrong.**

Every filter applied was commercial — market size, margin, MOQ, shipping weight, regulatory load, repeat purchase. Not one of them asked the question that actually decides a one-person business with €300:

> **Will this person still be doing the unglamorous work in month four?**

The recommended plan was *"lead with Dead Grip, deliver through clubs."* Club distribution means months of cold outreach to Spanish and Italian padel clubs, from Romania, by one person. That is a plan that only survives on conviction — and the client has no conviction in padel, which the nearest available player actively disliked.

**A plan requiring sustained effort in a category the operator does not care about is not a plan. It is a countdown.**

## The criterion that was missing

For the next selection, operator fit is weighted **above** market size and margin. Concretely:

| Weight | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **Highest** | Does the operator have genuine interest, access or knowledge in this category? |
| High | Can it survive on channels the operator will actually use? |
| High | Consumable or repeat purchase |
| Medium | Margin structure, shipping, MOQ, regulation |
| Lower | Total market size |

Market size was the first filter applied in the padel selection. It should have been close to the last. A €300 operator does not need a large market — they need a market they can get to, in a category they will not abandon.

Closed: the product decision, brief, and three directions.
