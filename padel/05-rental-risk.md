# Signal — The Rental Problem

**Source:** primary. The user's brother-in-law, an active padel player in Romania, reports that clubs rent racket and equipment for **~15 RON (≈€3) for 3 hours**, and that buying your own gear therefore is not worth it.

**Date logged:** before any money was spent. This is the intake process doing its job.

---

## Why this is serious

**A player who does not own a racket never buys an overgrip.** There is no version of this product that reaches a renter. The entire thesis assumes racket ownership as a precondition, and that assumption was never tested — it was inherited from the category, not verified.

**The comparison is brutal.** Renting a complete setup for three hours costs about the same as **one overgrip at retail** (€2–4). For a casual player in Romania, ownership is not merely unattractive — it is economically irrational. Our product sits on the far side of a purchase decision that the market is actively pricing people out of making.

**It also breaks the market sizing.** Every number in `01-product-decision.md` counts *courts* and *players*. Neither is the addressable market. The real one is:

> players who own a racket **and** play often enough to wear grips out

Nobody publishes that number. `[VERIFIED absence — searched, no published ownership rate exists]`

## What the research does and does not support

| Finding | Status |
|---|---|
| Most newcomers rent rather than buy | VERIFIED — consistent across sources |
| Players who commit to the sport transition to owning | VERIFIED but **qualitative only** |
| Entry-level rackets cost $50–100 | VERIFIED |
| **What share of regular players own a racket** | **NO DATA EXISTS.** The single number the business depends on |

## The honest reading

**This does not kill the thesis. It exposes the load-bearing unknown underneath it.**

One data point, from Romania — a market already identified as too small to sell into — from a player who by his own description is not the committed amateur the brief targets. That is not a falsification.

But it is a real warning, for a reason that goes beyond one person:

**If a meaningful share of people who look like padel fans in ad targeting are renters, then paid acquisition is partly buying impressions from people who structurally cannot buy.** At €13.64 contribution there is no room to absorb that. It would not show up as "the ads don't work" — it would show up as a CAC that never comes down no matter how good the creative is, which is the most expensive way to learn something.

It also, incidentally, **strengthens the club-channel recommendation**: at a club you can physically see who walks in carrying their own racket.

## What we do — cost €0, time ~1 week

**Do not build anything else until this is answered.** Phase 4 is suspended.

| # | Test | Cost | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email 5–8 clubs in **Spain and Italy**: *"roughly what share of your regular members bring their own racket?"* Clubs know this precisely and have no reason to lie | €0 | Draft: me · Send: you |
| 2 | Same question to 2–3 Romanian clubs, to size the gap between markets | €0 | You |
| 3 | Read r/padel and Spanish padel forums on when players buy their first racket | €0 | Me |
| 4 | Check whether Playtomic bookings show racket rental as a paid add-on, and how often | €0 | Me |

**Decision rule, fixed now:**

- **Ownership among regular players looks like 50%+ in Spain/Italy** → thesis holds, resume Phase 4, and target owners explicitly in all creative
- **Ownership looks like 20–50%** → thesis survives only through clubs, never paid. Direction B becomes the whole plan, not the channel
- **Ownership looks below 20%** → **the product is wrong. Stop and repick.** I will say so plainly rather than proposing we test it another way

## What changes in the existing work

- `01-product-decision.md` — risk table understated this. "Commodity, easily copied" was listed as the medium risk; **rental displacement is larger and was missing entirely.**
- `03-brief.md` — the audience definition already excludes renters, which is correct, but the brief never asked how many people that exclusion removes.
- `04-directions.md` — approval gate stays closed. Direction B's relative standing improves regardless of outcome.

## The process note

This is the fourteenth time in this engagement that building something surfaced a defect, and the first time the defect came from a real person rather than from the work. **One conversation with one actual player did more damage to a bad assumption than three phases of internal review.**

That is an argument for asking more players, sooner — not for asking fewer.
