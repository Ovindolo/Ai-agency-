# The Cost Stack — Romania Base, EU Market

Every figure is tagged. **VERIFIED** = from a cited source. **ESTIMATE** = my category judgment, no quote behind it. **MUST QUOTE** = we do not know and cannot guess responsibly.

---

## 1. The strategic split Romania forces

Romania is a **good place to operate from and a small place to sell into.**

Bucharest has around **11 clubs with padel courts**; the Romanian Padel Federation only affiliated to the International Padel Federation in **2025**, and courts are still appearing in Timișoara, Cluj, Constanța, Iași, Brașov. `[VERIFIED]` That is a sport arriving, not a sport arrived.

Meanwhile Spain has ~17,300 courts and ~6 million players, and Italy passed 10,000 courts in 2025. `[VERIFIED]`

**So: operate from Romania, sell into Spain and Italy.** Romania gives you a 1% or 3% turnover tax, an €80k VAT-free runway and low overheads. Spain and Italy give you customers. Those are different jobs and there is no reason they have to happen in the same country.

The cost of that split is cross-border shipping, which is the single biggest unknown in this document.

## 4. Romanian business setup

| Line | Figure | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| SRL registration, via an agency or lawyer | **€500–1,500, 3–5 days** | VERIFIED |
| SRL registration, filed yourself at ONRC | Substantially less | **MUST VERIFY** |
| Minimum share capital, new SRL from 2026 | **RON 500** (~€100) — capital, not a fee; it stays yours | VERIFIED |
| Share capital if turnover exceeds RON 400,000 | Rises to RON 5,000 | VERIFIED |
| **Micro-enterprise tax** | **1% of turnover with 1 employee, 3% without** | VERIFIED |
| Micro-enterprise revenue ceiling, 2026 | €100,000 | VERIFIED |
| **VAT registration threshold** | **RON 395,000** (~€79,000) — optional below it | VERIFIED |
| Romanian standard VAT | 21% | VERIFIED |

**Two things here are unusually good for you.** A 1–3% tax on *turnover* rather than profit is a genuinely favourable regime for a thin-margin ecommerce business. And an ~€79,000 VAT-free runway means early sales carry no VAT at all.

**One thing needs an accountant, not me.** Selling B2C across EU borders normally triggers the EU distance-selling rules and OSS registration once cross-border sales pass €10,000/year — and how that interacts with being *under* the Romanian VAT threshold is exactly the kind of question where a confident wrong answer costs real money. **Ask a Romanian accountant. Do not take my word for it, and do not take a forum's.**

## 5. The model — a 10-pack at €29

Using the estimates above. Change any input and this moves.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Price | €29.00 |
| Product, 10 × €0.70 | −€7.00 |
| Packaging | −€0.80 |
| Shipping to Spain/Italy | −€6.00 |
| Payment processing, ~1.5% + €0.25 | −€0.69 |
| Micro-enterprise tax, 3% of turnover | −€0.87 |
| **Contribution** | **€13.64** |

**That is your first-order CAC ceiling: about €13.64.**

### And here is the uncomfortable part

**€13.64 is probably not enough to run cold paid social on.** A new brand with no proof, no reviews and no recognition typically pays more than that to acquire a first customer on Meta. Not always — but often enough that building the plan around it would be dishonest.

**This contradicts the playbook.** The last campaign was paid-social-led because the economics supported it. These economics do not, and the right response is to change the plan rather than to hope.

**What the numbers actually point at:**

| Channel | Why it fits these economics |
|---|---|
| **Clubs** | 11 in Bucharest, thousands across Spain and Italy. Each is a room full of exactly one customer type. A club deal moves 50 units in one conversation |
| **Community and organic** | Padel content on Instagram and TikTok is enormous and under-monetised. Costs time, not CAC |
| **Subscription** | The wear cycle is physical and predictable. Retention is where this model makes its money, not acquisition |
| **Bigger first order** | A 24-grip season pack at ~€55 roughly doubles contribution and makes paid viable. Worth testing |
| Paid social | Later, once we know the real repeat rate and can justify the spend against LTV rather than first order |

I would rather tell you this now, before you have spent €300 finding it out.

## 6. What we do not know, ranked

1. **Cross-border shipping cost.** Moves contribution by several euros per order. Biggest single unknown.
2. **Real custom-print cost at 300–500 units.** The €0.50–0.90 estimate could be wrong in either direction.
3. **Whether a low-MOQ supplier will do a genuinely good grip.** Cheap grips exist. Good cheap grips are the question, and only a sample answers it.
4. **The OSS/VAT interaction.** Accountant.
5. **Real DIY SRL cost.** The €500–1,500 figure is agency pricing.

## 7. Next actions

**Mine, now, no money:**
- Draft the supplier RFQ (below) and a shortlist of 5–8 suppliers to send it to
- Draft the carrier rate-request email
- Build the live cost model so you can move the inputs as real quotes land

**Yours, when you want to:**
- Send the RFQ to the shortlist. Free, and it collapses unknowns 1–3 in about a week
- Ask the carriers for rates
- One call with a Romanian accountant about SRL vs PFA, micro-enterprise and OSS

**Note on sequencing:** SRL registration at €500–1,500 is more than your entire validation budget. **Do not register a company yet.** Validate with a waitlist that collects emails and takes no money — that needs no legal entity. Register once there is something to register for.

## Supplier RFQ — ready to send

> **Subject:** Padel overgrip — custom print, sample request and pricing for 300/500/1000 units
>
> Hello,
>
> I am sourcing padel overgrips for a new European brand and would like to request pricing and samples.
>
> **Specification**
> - Material: PU or PU blend, tacky finish
> - Thickness: 0.6–0.8mm
> - Length: 1100mm minimum
> - Perforated and non-perforated options, if available
> - Custom printed logo, single colour
> - Individually wrapped
>
> **Please quote**
> 1. Unit price at 300, 500 and 1,000 pieces, custom printed
> 2. Your minimum order quantity for custom printing
> 3. Sample cost and lead time — I would like to order samples before committing
> 4. Production lead time after order confirmation
> 5. Shipping cost and method to Romania, EU
> 6. Available colours, and whether Pantone matching is possible
> 7. Whether you can supply printed retail packaging
>
> I am starting small with the intention of reordering regularly. I would rather build a long relationship with one supplier than chase the lowest price once.
>
> Thank you,
> [your name]

Send it to 6–8 suppliers. Expect 3–4 replies and a wide price spread. **Order samples from at least three** — you cannot sell a grip you have not held, and the difference between a good and a bad overgrip is entirely in the hand.
