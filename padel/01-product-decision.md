# The Product Decision

**Padel accessories, built around the overgrip as the core consumable. Private label, not dropshipping. EU market.**

---

## Why padel

**The sport is genuinely booming and Europe is where it is happening.** As of the 2026 Global Padel Report there are roughly **58,300 courts, ~20,000 clubs and 19.4 million players worldwide**, and **Europe holds around 51,000 of those courts — about two-thirds of the global total.** Spain alone has ~17,300 courts and roughly 6 million players; Italy passed 10,000 courts in June 2025. The industry is put at about €2bn annually, growing ~15% a year. `[Sources below — verify before any of this appears in copy.]`

That matters for one specific reason: **a new sport has no incumbent accessory brands with emotional ownership.** Tennis has had decades to settle. Padel has not.

## Why the overgrip specifically

**It is a true consumable with a short, forced replacement cycle.**

- An intensive player training 3–4× a week gets through one every **2–3 weeks**
- A weekend player, every **4–6 weeks**
- Heat and heavy sweat halve those numbers
- Pros change them every match

That is a repeat purchase driven by physical wear, not by marketing. It is the single most important property a first product can have, and it is why I am not recommending rackets, bags or apparel — all one-time purchases, all inventory-heavy, all sized.

**Secondary properties that make it the right first product:**

| Property | Why it matters at €350 of exposure |
|---|---|
| Tiny unit cost | Private label is affordable at low volume |
| Low MOQ | Typically a few hundred units, not thousands |
| Weightless | Letterbox shipping across the EU |
| No sizing | One SKU fits every customer. No dead stock |
| Unregulated | No CE, no expiry, no cosmetics rules, no food safety |
| No tech risk | Nothing to fail, nothing to return under warranty |

## Why private label and not dropshipping

You said dropshipping. I want to push back on one word of that, then agree with the spirit of it.

**Dropshipping generic overgrips is not a business.** You would be selling an unbranded product, visible on AliExpress at a fifth of your price, with 10–20 day shipping and no control over quality. Every customer who searches finds the source. There is no repeat purchase because there is nothing to be loyal to.

**But the thing you actually want from dropshipping — no inventory risk, low startup cost — is achievable here anyway**, because overgrip unit economics are so forgiving. A few hundred branded units is a small order. You get: your name on it, your packaging, EU shipping in days, control over the product, and something a customer can be loyal to.

So: **validate with no inventory, then go branded.** Same risk profile, actual business at the end of it.

## The white space

The category today is **retailers and racket brands**. Bullpadel, Adidas, Head, NOX, Babolat, Wilson all sell grips — as an afterthought to the racket. Retailers stock everyone's. There are small players (Get A Grip Club appears to be one) but nothing that reads as *the* grip brand with a point of view.

Nobody has built the grip-first brand for padel. That is the opening.

## The central commercial problem — read this before we go further

**The price point is the hard part, and it is the thing the entire campaign has to solve.**

A single overgrip sells for roughly €2–4. A three-pack, €8–12. **You cannot pay €10 to acquire a customer who spends €10.** Paid social maths does not work on a €10 order, full stop.

This is the equivalent of AIRING's invisible-benefit problem: the one structural obstacle that everything else is designed around. If we do not solve it, nothing else matters.

**The three ways out, which the offer design has to pick from:**

1. **Multi-pack as the entry unit** — sell 10 or 12, not 3. Pushes the first order toward €25–35, which is a workable number.
2. **Subscription as the default** — grips arrive when they wear out, because the wear cycle is predictable. This is the strongest structural fit I have seen for a subscription in a long time: the customer genuinely does need a new one every few weeks, and genuinely does forget.
3. **Bundle beyond the grip** — grip enhancer, racket protector, ball tube, towel. Raises AOV without adding sizing or inventory complexity.

My instinct is all three, with the subscription as the hero. But that is a Phase 3 decision and I am not making it in a product-decision document.

## Risks, stated plainly

| Risk | Severity | What we do about it |
|---|---|---|
| **AOV too low for paid acquisition** | **High — the main one** | Offer design above. If Stage 2 shows we cannot get orders above ~€25, the model is wrong and we stop |
| Court growth outpacing player growth | Medium | The 2026 report flags overcapacity in some markets. Affects club operators more than accessory sellers, but it means the "endless growth" story is not one we should tell |
| Racket brands have club distribution | Medium | We do not fight for shelf space. Direct, online, community-led |
| Product feels cheap in hand | Medium | Stage 1 exists precisely to find this out for €40 |
| Commodity — easily copied | Medium | True. The defensibility is brand and subscription retention, not the product |

## Alternatives I rejected

- **Pickleball accessories** — same logic, wrong continent. It is a North American sport. You are in the EU.
- **Espresso puck prep tools** — good margins, but one-time purchase and a crowded field of established specialist brands.
- **Anything supplements, skincare or electronics** — regulatory load and return rates that a €350 budget cannot absorb.
- **Apparel** — sizing, dead stock, returns. The worst possible first product for a small budget.
