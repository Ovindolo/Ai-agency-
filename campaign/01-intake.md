## 1. What are we selling?

**AIRING No.1 Refresh Mist** — a garment refresh mist that removes odor from clothes so they can be worn again without washing.

- **Format:** 250ml matte aluminum bottle, black fine-mist pump actuator. Refilled by a 30ml concentrate vial + tap water.
- **Mechanism:** an enzyme blend that breaks down odor-causing compounds, plus a molecular-capture agent that traps volatile odor molecules. It removes odor rather than perfuming over it. No fabric softeners, no quaternary ammonium compounds, no residue — so it is safe on merino, cashmere, raw denim and elastane.
- **Scents:** *Cold Air* (neutral, no added fragrance) and *Line Dry* (very light clean-cotton mineral).
- **Price:** Starter Kit $34 (bottle + 1 concentrate). Refill 3-pack $27. Subscription −15%.
- **Delivery:** DTC ecommerce, Shopify. Refills ship letterbox-size.

**Why this business:** a consumable with a repeat cycle built into the product architecture, high gross margin, low shipping weight on the refill, an expansion path (Amazon → Huckberry/REI/Nordstrom → EU), and a real behavioral wedge rather than a formulation gimmick.

## 2. Who is the ideal customer?

**Primary — "The Considered Wardrobe."** 27–44, urban and inner-suburban, household income $90k+. In the last three years they deliberately shifted to buying fewer, better clothes: merino tees, raw denim, cashmere knits, technical outerwear, well-made basics. They can tell you what a garment cost and where it came from.

What they already do: they re-wear. Quietly, and with a low hum of anxiety about it. They sniff the armpit before committing.

What they already believe: dry cleaning is overpriced and probably harmful. Febreze is chemical masking for people who don't care. The washing machine is neutral — it is simply what you do with clothes.

That last belief is the one the campaign has to break.

Where their attention is: Instagram Reels, TikTok (menswear, #capsulewardrobe, thrift and denim communities), YouTube (garment reviewers, "what I wore this week"), Reddit (r/malefashionadvice, r/femalefashionadvice, r/rawdenim, r/BuyItForLife), style Substacks.

**Secondary:** one-bag travelers and frequent fliers; parents doing triage laundry; anyone whose wardrobe is now mostly wool or technical activewear.

## 3. What painful or expensive problem does it solve — and what do they want instead?

**In their words:** *"I paid $120 for a merino tee and it pilled, stretched and went out of shape in four months. And I still feel gross wearing it two days running."*

Two pains, stacked:
- **Financial** — good clothes degrade fast and get replaced far sooner than their price implied.
- **Social** — re-wearing feels slightly shameful, so they wash defensively, which accelerates the first pain. The pain is a loop.

**What they want:** clothes that still look new in year three, and permission to re-wear without thinking about it.

## 4. Why us instead of the alternative?

The alternatives are wash it (destructive), dry-clean it (expensive, slow, solvent-heavy), or spray a fabric deodorizer (masking, residue, softener-coated fibers).

Our claim is a *mechanism* claim, not a superiority adjective: we remove the odor compound; we do not add a stronger smell on top of it. And we are formulated around fabric safety first — no softeners or quats that coat and weaken fibers over time.

Honest assessment: the formulation is licensable chemistry, not patentable magic. The defensible asset is the **behavioral reframe** — *washing is what's wearing your clothes out* — plus the brand, the ritual and the refill subscription. We compete on category leadership, not molecules.

## 5. What single action should the campaign drive?

**Purchase of the $34 Starter Kit**, with subscription offered post-purchase rather than in the primary ad CTA.

## 6. Which assets first, and by when?

1. Paid social video (Meta + TikTok) — the volume driver
2. UGC scripts — feeds #1
3. Landing page — the conversion floor
4. Product photography — feeds #1, #3 and retail
5. Organic social — the brand layer

Assumed timeline: 6 weeks concept → live. Assumed test budget: modest, structured as hypothesis tests rather than a scaled spend.

## 7. Platform and format constraints

- Meta Feed/Reels and TikTok: 9:16 primary, 1:1 secondary, 4:5 for feed statics
- Hook must land in the first 2 seconds; audio-off legibility required
- Category: household/fabric care. Not a drug, not a cosmetic. But odor, fabric-safety and environmental claims are all advertising-substantiation territory and are treated as such throughout.

## 8. References available

**None supplied.** The Brand Lock in Phase 4 therefore defines the brand from scratch and becomes the BRAND REF for all future generation. Product, scene and UGC references must be shot or generated once and then frozen — see `07-handoff.md`, production order step 1.

## 9. What must never change?

Locked in full in `04-brand-lock.md`. In short: the name AIRING, the product name No.1 Refresh Mist, the bottle (matte aluminum, flat shoulder, black pump actuator), the single-line wordmark at the bottle's midpoint, the scent names *Cold Air* and *Line Dry*, the palette, and the tagline *Wear it again.*

## 10. What real proof exists?

**None. The brand does not exist.** There are zero customers, zero reviews, zero test results, zero certifications and zero partnerships — and none are invented anywhere in this package. Every asset that would normally carry proof carries a marked placeholder instead, and every factual claim is registered in `claims-substantiation-register.md` with the evidence required to make it sayable.
