What the model says

Four things worth acting on

Correction

The refill isn't "the business." It's half of it.

Earlier in this engagement the agency wrote that the refill is the business. At these assumptions that's wrong: the starter kit contributes $17.61 on its own — 54% of twelve-month value. That's a structurally stronger position than a loss-leader model, because survival doesn't depend on retention. It also means the acquisition ad can be judged on first-order economics, which is a much faster feedback loop than waiting on a repeat cohort.

Lever

Fulfillment is the second-largest cost on the starter.

At $5.40 it is 15.9% of the starter price — more than processing and returns combined. The free-shipping threshold sits at $30 so the $34 kit always absorbs it. Moving that threshold above the starter price would recover the margin and kill conversion; the real lever is packaging the starter to ship at letterbox rate, which is a product decision, not a marketing one.

Decision

The subscription discount has a hurdle rate.

A 15% subscribe-and-save cuts refill contribution from $18.62 to $14.68. To be worth offering, it must lift refill volume by at least 26.8%. That is a testable number, not a preference — and it should be tested before the discount is printed on the page, because a discount is far easier to launch than to withdraw.

Gap — F-14

Nothing in this package earns the refill.

46% of twelve-month value arrives after the ad has done its job, and the campaign specifies no post-purchase flow at all — no refill reminder timed to the bottle running out, no first-use email that improves the odds the product works, no replenishment trigger. Seven organic posts were specified for people who haven't bought, and zero touches for people who have. That is a real hole in the work, found by modeling it, and it is now logged against the campaign.

A structure, not a forecast. Every input ships with an agency assumption because no real figure exists — the brand has no supplier quotes, no shipped orders and no repeat cohort. Nothing here predicts performance, and no output should be quoted as a projection.

What it does do is convert "we don't set CPA targets before launch" into "here is how you derive yours, and here is which input moves it most." Replace the assumptions with quotes and cohort data and the outputs become decision-grade.
