# 4. Advertising

> **Every concept below is a hypothesis, not a promise.** No performance is predicted, guaranteed or implied. Each unit states what it is testing so that a result — win or lose — teaches something. Claims marked `[RTB-n]` cannot run until cleared in `claims-substantiation-register.md`.

---

## The five hooks

Built to attack five different entry points, not five phrasings of one.

| # | Hook | Entry point | Awareness level | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **"Your washing machine is the most destructive appliance you own."** `[RTB-1]` | The enemy | Problem-unaware | Whether naming a villain can create a problem where the viewer felt none |
| H2 | **"That sweater didn't wear out. You washed it out."** `[RTB-1]` | Personal loss | Problem-aware | Whether a specific grievance outperforms a general accusation |
| H3 | **"Wool doesn't need washing. It needs airing."** | Category knowledge | Solution-aware | Whether a fabric-nerd fact recruits the denim/wool community as a beachhead |
| H4 | **"I haven't washed these jeans since March."** | Social proof of behavior | Product-unaware | Whether the confession format out-hooks the argument format |
| H5 | **"Fabric spray doesn't clean anything. Here's what it actually does."** | Skepticism | Most aware | Whether meeting the objection head-on converts the people the other four lose |

H5 is the important one. It concedes the category's weakness in the first line, which is the fastest route to credibility with the exact person most likely to buy.

## Three static ad concepts

### S-01 — "Same Sweater"
- **Visual required.** PS-03 "The Evidence," 1:1 or 4:5. Caution Orange annotation set in layout.
- **Headline.** `Same sweater. One of these went in the machine.`
- **Primary text.** *Pilling isn't age. It's abrasion — forty minutes of wet fiber dragging against wet fiber, on repeat.* `[RTB-1]` *Most of the time your clothes aren't dirty. They just smell. AIRING is an enzyme mist that removes the odor compounds instead of covering them, so the shirt goes back in the wardrobe instead of the drum.* `[RTB-2]`
- **Supporting copy / caption.** `Wear it again.`
- **CTA.** `Get the Starter Kit — $34`
- **Awareness level.** Problem-unaware → problem-aware.
- **Hypothesis.** *If we show fiber damage before we mention odor, cold traffic will engage with a laundry product it would otherwise scroll past.* Measured on hook rate and outbound CTR against a product-first control.

### S-02 — "The Drum"
- **Visual required.** Washing machine drum interior, cold, hard light, shot from inside the door. Graphite-dominant, 4:5. Type reversed out in Paper, flush left in the upper third.
- **Headline.** `Forty minutes of this. Twice a week.`
- **Primary text.** *Nobody questions the machine. It's just what you do with clothes. But agitation and heat are what turn a good knit into a tired one* `[RTB-1]` *— and most loads go in for smell, not dirt. AIRING removes the smell. Enzymes, not fragrance. No softeners, no residue, safe on merino, cashmere and raw denim.* `[RTB-2]` `[RTB-3]`
- **CTA.** `Get the Starter Kit — $34`
- **Awareness level.** Problem-unaware.
- **Hypothesis.** *If the product is absent from the image entirely, the argument lands harder and the click is more qualified.* Measured on add-to-cart rate, not CTR alone — a lower, better-qualified click is a win here.

### S-03 — "Day Nine"
- **Visual required.** PS-02 "Day Two," recomposed with indigo denim over a chair back; mono day-count set in layout upper-left.
- **Headline.** `Day nine. Nobody noticed.`
- **Primary text.** *Raw denim isn't meant to go in the machine — water and agitation take the indigo and the fit with them. So what do you do instead? Two sprays, inside the waistband and the back of the knee. Hang it up. AIRING is an enzyme mist that breaks odor compounds down rather than layering fragrance on top.* `[RTB-2]`
- **CTA.** `Get the Starter Kit — $34`
- **Awareness level.** Solution-aware.
- **Hypothesis.** *A narrow, community-specific ad will beat the broad ad within the denim/wool audience by enough to justify running audience-specific creative rather than one universal asset.*

## Three short-form video concepts

### V-01 — "Two Sweaters" · 15s · 9:16
| Time | Visual | Audio / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:02 | Hard cut to macro pilling, light raking | VO: *"This one's been washed."* |
| 0:02–0:04 | Whip to clean knit, identical light | VO: *"This one hasn't."* |
| 0:04–0:07 | Slow push on the drum interior, cold | VO: *"Same sweater. The difference is the machine."* `[RTB-1]` |
| 0:07–0:12 | Bottle picked up off a dresser, two sprays across a hanging tee | VO: *"Most washes are for smell, not dirt. This removes the smell — enzymes, not fragrance."* `[RTB-2]` |
| 0:12–0:15 | Tee on a hanger, morning light, holds | Text: `wear it again.` → `Get the Starter Kit — $34` |

- **Awareness.** Problem-unaware. **Sound-off legible:** yes, full burned-in captions, mono, lower third.
- **Hypothesis.** *The two-sweater comparison in the first two seconds will hold cold traffic past three seconds at a materially higher rate than a talking-head open.* This is the lead test of the whole launch.

### V-02 — "The Objection" · 20s · 9:16
Opens on H5 — *"Fabric spray doesn't clean anything."* — spoken flat to camera, then earns its way back over 20 seconds: what odor actually is, why masking fails, what enzymes do, what the product does **not** do. Ends on the honest boundary: *"It doesn't replace washing. It replaces the washes you didn't need."*
- **Awareness.** Most aware / skeptical. **Hypothesis.** *Leading with the category's weakness will outperform leading with the benefit on a warm retargeting audience,* measured on ROAS within the 7-day retarget pool.
- **Note.** This is also the legal safe-harbour asset — it is the only unit that explicitly bounds the claim, and it should be the first thing counsel reviews.

### V-03 — "The Five Feet" · 12s · 9:16
No voiceover. Sound design only: wardrobe door, two trigger pulls, hanger on a rail, a door closing. Shot as one continuous 12-second move through the five feet between the wardrobe and the front door. Type enters twice: `most loads go in for smell, not dirt` at 0:05, `wear it again.` at 0:10.
- **Awareness.** Solution-aware, retargeting, and brand. **Hypothesis.** *A non-verbal, texture-led unit will hold a warm audience and lift branded search even at a weaker direct-response rate.* Judged on frequency-adjusted brand search lift, not CPA.

## Test structure

Run as three sequential questions, not one big bake-off. Nothing here predicts a result.

**Test 1 — Does the argument work?** V-01 vs. a product-first control. Question: does the fiber-damage frame buy attention from a cold stranger?
**Test 2 — Which entry point?** H1 / H2 / H3 / H4 as four cuts of the winning V-01 body. Question: enemy, loss, knowledge, or confession?
**Test 3 — Does the objection convert?** V-02 vs. V-01 on warm retargeting. Question: does honesty beat benefit on the skeptic?

**Kill criteria are set with the media buyer against real spend floors, not asserted here.** Any agency quoting you a target CPA before a single impression has run is guessing, and a guess in a deck becomes a promise in a meeting.
