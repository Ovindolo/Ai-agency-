The two decisions

The cold open

No brand, no product, no context for two full seconds — just fabric. Cold traffic gives roughly that long, and a logo spends it on something the viewer has no reason to care about yet.

Frame 2 is the whole ad

If shots 1 and 2 aren't lit identically, at the same scale, on the same fabric, the comparison is worthless — and a viewer will feel the cheat without being able to name it. Shoot both halves in one session without moving the light.

Test 1

What this unit is asking

HypothesisThe two-sweater comparison in the first two seconds will hold cold traffic past three seconds at a materially higher rate than a talking-head open.
AgainstA product-first control — same copy, same offer, opening on the bottle.
Read on3-second hold rate first, outbound CTR second. Not CPA — Test 1 is an attention question, and judging it on conversion answers a different one.

No outcome is predicted. If the hypothesis fails, the finding is that the argument doesn't buy attention in this category — which is worth knowing before a scaled spend, and is the entire reason the test runs first.

Production

What must be shot, and what may be generated

Shots 1 and 2 must be photographed. They are the campaign's evidence. Generating the proof for a brand built on refusing fake proof is the worst available own-goal — and it's the one place in this package where a synthetic asset is an integrity problem, not a production shortcut.

GenerateThe drum interior, V-02's fabric macros, all B-roll. None of it carries evidentiary weight.
Shoot orderBoth knit halves first, one session, light untouched between them. Then the drum. Then product interaction. The hanging-tee close reuses the PS-02 setup — same day.
CaptionsJetBrains Mono, lowercase, Graphite on a Paper pad at 82%, lower third, flush left, 3.3% of frame height. Never over a face, never centered.

The other two units

V-02 and V-03

V-02 — "The Objection" · 20s

Opens by conceding the category's weakness — "Fabric spray doesn't clean anything" — then earns its way back: what odor is, why masking fails, what enzymes do, and what the product does not do.

Closes on the boundary line: "It doesn't replace washing. It replaces the washes you didn't need."

Review this one first. It's the only unit that explicitly bounds the claim, and that closing sentence has to survive legal verbatim — it's what keeps the whole campaign inside what it can defend.

V-03 — "The Five Feet" · 12s

One continuous move through the five feet between the wardrobe and the front door. No voiceover. Sound design only: hinge, hanger, two pump hisses, a door closing.

The risk, stated plainly. A unit with no argument in it will almost certainly underperform V-01 on direct response. It isn't built for that. Judge it on frequency-adjusted branded search lift — and kill it if that read isn't available, because a brand unit you can't measure is one you can't defend in a budget meeting.

Animatic, not footage. Every frame is drawn procedurally in canvas as a timing and layout study. Nothing here is a photograph, nothing is a real garment, and none of it is evidence of anything.

What it is good for: judging pace, caption timing, the audio cut at 0:07, and whether the hook survives its two-second window. That is what the paper storyboard could not tell you.

RTB-1 and RTB-2 both gate this unit. It cannot run until they clear.
