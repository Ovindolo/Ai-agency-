## THE MECHANISM

### Most of your laundry isn't dirty. It smells.

Two different problems, one solution applied to both.

Dirt needs washing. Nothing removes a stain but water and agitation, and AIRING will not pretend otherwise.

Odor is a chemical compound sitting in the fiber. It can be broken down where it is.

**How AIRING works — three steps**

**1 · Enzymes break the compound down.** The odor molecules that bind to wool and synthetic fiber are digested, not perfumed over. `[RTB-2]`

**2 · Molecular capture traps what's left.** Remaining volatile compounds are held rather than released. `[RTB-2]`

**3 · Nothing stays behind.** No softeners, no quaternary ammonium compounds, no residue that coats fiber and dulls it over time. Safe on merino, cashmere, raw denim and elastane. `[RTB-3]`

> **What it doesn't do.** It doesn't clean. It doesn't remove stains. It doesn't sanitize. It replaces the washes that were only ever about smell — which, for most wardrobes, is most of them.

## HOW IT WORKS

**1 · Spray.** Two to four pulls, 20cm from the fabric. The high-odor spots: underarms, collar, waistband, back of the knee.
**2 · Hang.** Anywhere with moving air. A door frame is fine.

## OBJECTION HANDLING

**"Isn't this just Febreze?"**
No — and you're right to ask, because most of this category is fragrance. Fragrance covers odor with a stronger smell. AIRING is enzyme-based: it breaks the odor compound down and captures what's left. `[RTB-2]` If you want the honest test, don't smell the garment while it's damp. Smell it tomorrow.

**"Isn't it unhygienic to not wash clothes?"**
Underwear, socks and anything directly against skin for a full day should be washed. Outer layers — knitwear, jeans, jackets, outer training layers — are a different case, and most of them go in the machine because of smell rather than soil. That's the load AIRING is for.

**"Will it damage wool or cashmere?"**
It's formulated around those fibers specifically: no softeners, no quats, no residue. `[RTB-3]` Patch-test a hem if you're unsure — with anything you apply to a garment you care about, you should.

**"Does it actually work?"**
We'd rather you find out than take our word for it, and we haven't got the customer history yet to point at. That's what the 30 days are for. Use it for a month. If your laundry pile hasn't shrunk, send it back.

**"What if I hate the smell?"**
*Cold Air* has no added fragrance at all. Start there.

## Build notes

- **One CTA, five appearances,** identical wording every time: hero, after mechanism, after objections, offer block, final. No secondary CTA anywhere on the page.
- **Above the fold on mobile** must contain: H1, subhead, CTA, price. Nothing else competes.
- **Type minimums:** 17px body on mobile, 1.5 line height, 65–75 character measure. Never type on a photograph without a solid contrast pad.
- **Proof section renders conditionally.** Build it as a component that returns null when the data source is empty, so it is structurally impossible to ship a fake one.
- **Every `[RTB-n]` string is a build-blocker.** The page does not deploy while one is unresolved. Wire this as a CI check, not a checklist item — a checklist is a thing people tick, and a build failure is a thing people fix.
