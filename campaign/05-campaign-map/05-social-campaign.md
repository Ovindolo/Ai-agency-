# 6. Organic Social — Seven-Post Launch Campaign

One position, seven entry points, seven different hooks. Sequenced over ten days. Every post reinforces *washing is the damage* without repeating the same opening move.

Platform: Instagram (Reels + carousel) primary, TikTok mirror, LinkedIn for post 5 only.

---

### POST 1 — AWARENESS
**Format:** Reel, 12s, no voiceover
**Hook:** *(text on a black frame, 0:00)* `the most destructive appliance in your home`
**Body:** Slow push into a washing machine drum. Cut to macro pilling. Cut to the same knit intact. Cut to black.
**Caption:**
> Most people have never once wondered whether the machine is the problem.
> Forty minutes of wet fiber dragging against wet fiber, twice a week, is what turns a good knit into a tired one. `[RTB-1]`
> We're starting here because everything else we have to say depends on it.
> New: AIRING No.1. Link in bio.
**Why it's here:** plants the belief before the product exists in anyone's mind.

---

### POST 2 — PROBLEM
**Format:** Carousel, 5 slides
**Hook (slide 1):** `You didn't wear it out. You washed it out.`
**Slides:** 2 — macro pilled shoulder, Caution Orange annotation. 3 — *"Pilling is abrasion. Abrasion is agitation."* `[RTB-1]` 4 — *"Shape loss is heat plus water plus tumbling."* `[RTB-1]` 5 — *"Most of those loads went in because something smelled. Not because it was dirty."*
**Caption:**
> The loop nobody notices: it smells → you wash it → washing degrades it → it looks tired → you replace it.
> The expensive part of a wardrobe isn't the buying. It's the re-buying.
**Why it's here:** converts the abstract villain into the reader's own sweater.

---

### POST 3 — EDUCATIONAL
**Format:** Carousel, 6 slides — *"How often things actually need washing"*
**Hook (slide 1):** `Wool doesn't need washing. It needs airing.`
**Slides:** merino · raw denim · cashmere · technical synthetics · cotton tees · everything against skin — each with a qualitative answer (*"several wears," "most of a week," "every wear, no exceptions"*), never a wear-count figure.
**Caption:**
> Half of this list is things you're over-washing. The other half is things you shouldn't stretch, and we'll say so: underwear, socks, anything worn directly against skin for a full day — wash them.
> A brand that tells you to wash less has an obvious incentive to overstate it. So here's the version with the exceptions left in.
**Why it's here:** the most saveable, most shareable post in the set, and the honesty is the differentiator.
**Note:** the slides are written qualitatively by design. A numeric version of this carousel would perform better and cannot be published until wear-count guidance is sourced and cleared at `[RTB-1]` grade. Do not let a designer add numbers at artwork stage — this is the most likely place in the whole campaign for an invented statistic to slip in.

---

### POST 4 — DEMONSTRATION
**Format:** Reel, 20s, voiceover
**Hook:** *"You're testing fabric spray wrong."*
**Body:** The honest-test protocol from UGC-03 — don't smell it wet, smell the bag tomorrow. Shows the spray, the hanger, the hour.
**Caption:**
> Anything smells fine while it's damp with fragrance. That's not a test, that's a magic trick.
> Spray it. Hang it. Check it in the morning. If the smell is back, it was masked. If it isn't, it was removed. `[RTB-2]`
> That's the test we'd want if we were you.
**Why it's here:** hands the skeptic a falsifiable test — the strongest move available to a brand with no proof yet.

---

### POST 5 — BRAND STORY
**Format:** Single image + long caption (also LinkedIn)
**Hook:** `We are a new brand with no reviews, and we're not going to fake them.`
**Image:** PS-01, the bottle, hard light, nothing else.
**Caption:**
> AIRING launches today with zero customer reviews, zero press, and zero test results we can show you yet. Independent odor-panel testing is underway. `[RTB-2]` When it's finished we'll publish it whether or not we like it.
>
> In the meantime, here's what we'll stand behind now: the bottle is refillable aluminum, the formula has no softeners or quats in it, and it doesn't replace washing — it replaces the washes that were only ever about smell.
>
> And 30 days to change your mind, bottle empty.
>
> That's the whole pitch. We'd rather start slow and true than fast and borrowed.
**Why it's here:** for this audience, the absence of manufactured proof *is* the proof. This post is the brand's character.
**Note:** if a real founder exists, this becomes a founder post and gains substantially. Assumption A-08 flagged.

---

### POST 6 — OBJECTION HANDLING
**Format:** Reel, 25s, straight to camera
**Hook:** *"Fabric spray doesn't clean anything."*
**Body:** Concede it fully. Then separate dirt from odor, explain enzymes vs. fragrance, and state the boundary out loud: *"It doesn't replace washing. It replaces the washes you didn't need."*
**Caption:**
> Three objections we get, answered properly:
> 1. *It's just Febreze* — fragrance covers, enzymes remove. Different mechanism. `[RTB-2]`
> 2. *It's unhygienic* — for outer layers, no. For anything against skin all day, wash it.
> 3. *It'll wreck my cashmere* — no softeners, no quats, no residue. Patch-test a hem anyway. `[RTB-3]`
> If there's a fourth, ask it below and we'll answer it in public.
**Why it's here:** pre-empts the comment section by hosting it.

---

### POST 7 — OFFER
**Format:** Reel, 10s + pinned comment
**Hook:** `Day nine. Nobody noticed.`
**Body:** The UGC-02 mirror sequence, cut tight. Ends on the bottle and one line of type.
**Caption:**
> Starter Kit — $34. One refillable aluminum bottle, one concentrate, *Cold Air* (no fragrance) or *Line Dry*.
> 30 days to change your mind. Free shipping over $30.
> **Wear it again.** Link in bio.
**Why it's here:** the only hard-sell post in the set. It has earned the right by post 7 and would have been ignored at post 1.

## Sequencing

| Day | Post | Move |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 — Awareness | Plant the villain |
| 2 | 2 — Problem | Make it personal |
| 4 | 3 — Educational | Deliver value, earn the save |
| 6 | 4 — Demonstration | Hand over a falsifiable test |
| 7 | 5 — Brand story | Declare character |
| 9 | 6 — Objections | Clear the path |
| 10 | 7 — Offer | Ask |

**The rule underneath the sequence:** posts 1–6 give something away. Post 7 asks. Six-to-one is the ratio that earns a follow from an audience that can smell a funnel from the first frame.

## Hook variation check

Villain · loss · knowledge · protocol · confession · concession · result. Seven distinct opening moves, one position. No hook is reused across the set — which is the actual test of whether a social campaign is a campaign or a repost.
