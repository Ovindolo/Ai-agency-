# 2. AI UGC — Three Creator-Led Concepts

> **Labeling requirement, non-negotiable.** These are AI-generated concept demonstrations performed by synthetic creators. Nothing here is a real customer, a real result, or a real experience. Every published asset carries a visible `AI-generated` label in-frame and in caption per Meta and TikTok synthetic-media policy, and no script contains a first-person result claim ("it saved my sweater") that a real customer has not made. Scripts are written so the *creator states the mechanism and their intention*, never a personal outcome, and carry no specific unregistered figures — no prices, no wear counts, no timeframes presented as a lived result. A persistent in-frame `AI-generated · concept demonstration` label runs for the full duration of every asset, not just the end card. See `07-handoff.md` → Human Approval Gates.

Each concept below is production-ready: hook, persona, setting, script, shot list, on-screen text, B-roll, product interaction, CTA, and the generation prompts.

## UGC-01 — "The Drawer Audit"

**Hook (0:00–0:02).** *"I just found out I've been wrecking my own clothes. Not wearing them. Washing them."*

**Creator persona.** Early 30s, dry and slightly annoyed, the friend who researches things properly before buying. Not a hype account. Speaks like someone correcting a misunderstanding, including their own.

**Setting.** A real bedroom, mid-morning. Open dresser drawer, window light from camera left. Slightly untidy — a chair with clothes on it in the background, deliberately.

**Spoken script (≈28s).**
> "I just found out I've been wrecking my own clothes. Not wearing them — washing them.
> *(pulls out a merino tee, turns the shoulder to the light)*
> This is good merino. It has not had a hard life. Look at the shoulder.
> That's not wear. That's agitation — the machine dragging fiber against fiber, forty minutes at a time, wet and hot. `[RTB-1]`
> So the actual problem isn't dirt. Most of the time it's just smell.
> *(picks up AIRING, two sprays across the tee, hangs it on the door)*
> This is an enzyme spray — it breaks the odor compounds down instead of covering them with a stronger smell. `[RTB-2]` Spray, hang it up, wear it tomorrow.
> I'm not going to tell you it saved this one. That one's gone. I'm telling you what I'm doing with the next one."

**Shot list.**
1. 0:00–0:02 — Handheld MCU, creator to lens, drawer open behind. Hook delivered cold, no intro.
2. 0:02–0:07 — Cut to insert: hands lifting tee out of drawer, natural handheld drift.
3. 0:07–0:12 — **Macro insert.** Shoulder seam raked by window light, pills catching highlights. The money shot.
4. 0:12–0:18 — Back to MCU, creator explaining, tee held up in frame.
5. 0:18–0:24 — Product interaction: bottle picked up off dresser, two visible pump presses, fine fan of mist across the fabric.
6. 0:24–0:28 — Wide: tee on a wooden hanger on the door frame, creator steps out of frame, holds on the empty shot for a beat.

**On-screen text.** `0:01` *washing ≠ cleaning* · `0:08` *this is abrasion, not age* · `0:19` *enzyme, not fragrance* · `0:26` *wear it again.* (Graphite on Paper pad, mono, lower third, flush left.)

**B-roll to generate separately.** Macro pilling on merino · a knit cuff distorting under tension · a washing machine drum spinning, shot from inside the door, cold and unlovely · a hanger on a door frame in morning light.

**Product interaction rules.** Bottle lives on the dresser in shot before it is used. Pump pressed flat with the index finger, bottle held upright at 20–25cm from fabric. Mist reads as a fine fan that disperses, never as an aerosol cloud. Wordmark faces camera on pickup. Bottle is never waved at the lens.

**CTA.** Spoken: none. On-screen end card only: `Get the Starter Kit — $34` with `AI-generated` label persistent bottom-left.

## UGC-02 — "Nine Days"

**Hook (0:00–0:02).** *"These are day nine. Nobody has said anything. Here's the whole system."*

**Creator persona.** Late 20s, denim-literate, quietly confident, talks fast. The kind of person who knows what selvedge means and doesn't make it a personality.

**Setting.** A hallway mirror. Phone filming the reflection. Jacket on a hook behind, shoes lined up below.

**Spoken script (≈25s).**
> "These are day nine. Nobody's said anything.
> Raw denim isn't supposed to go in the machine — water and agitation kill the indigo and blow out the fit. Everyone who's into denim knows that. Nobody tells you what to do instead.
> Here's what I do instead.
> *(two sprays, inside of the waistband and the back of the knee)*
> Enzyme spray, then the balcony for an hour. It's the two spots that actually hold smell, and the enzymes break the compounds down — they're not sitting on top of it with fragrance. `[RTB-2]`
> Day nine. Still dark. Still fits.
> The machine was never the only option."

**Shot list.**
1. 0:00–0:03 — Mirror selfie, wide, full body, phone visible. Hook to reflection.
2. 0:03–0:08 — Insert: hand running down the outseam, indigo texture in raking light.
3. 0:08–0:14 — Talking over a **close insert of the waistband interior**, bottle entering frame.
4. 0:14–0:19 — Product interaction: two sprays, waistband then back of knee, mist visible as a fine fan.
5. 0:19–0:22 — Cut to balcony: jeans over a rail, backlit, clean silhouette.
6. 0:22–0:25 — Return to mirror, half-step turn, hold.

**On-screen text.** `0:00` *day 9* · `0:05` *raw denim ≠ machine* · `0:15` *waistband + back of knee* · `0:23` *wear it again.*

**B-roll.** Indigo weave macro · a hand flexing the knee of dry denim · jeans over a balcony rail at golden-adjacent but cool light · the bottle standing on a windowsill.

**Product interaction rules.** Sprayed on the *inside* of the waistband — specific, credible, and it is what a denim person would actually do. Bottle set down in frame, not held aloft.

## UGC-03 — "The Gym Bag Test"

**Hook (0:00–0:02).** *"Everyone's testing this wrong. Don't smell the shirt. Smell the bag."*

**Creator persona.** Mid 30s, trains seriously, practical to the point of blunt. Zero aesthetic interest in laundry; total interest in gear lasting.

**Setting.** A garage or entryway, evening. Gym bag on the floor, technical tee in hand, concrete and a bare bulb.

**Spoken script (≈30s).**
> "Everyone tests these sprays wrong. Don't smell the shirt right after — anything smells fine when it's wet with fragrance.
> Smell the *bag*. Smell it tomorrow.
> Synthetic training gear holds odor differently than cotton — the compounds bind to the fiber, which is why your tee comes out of the wash still smelling faintly of last Tuesday. `[RTB-2]`
> *(sprays the tee, front and back, hangs it on a nail)*
> This one's enzyme-based, so it breaks those compounds down rather than layering a smell over them. `[RTB-2]` And it doesn't have softeners in it, which is the thing that wrecks the stretch in technical fabric. `[RTB-3]`
> Hang it, give it an hour, check it tomorrow. That's the actual test.
> I'm not asking you to believe me. I'm telling you how to find out."

**Shot list.**
1. 0:00–0:03 — Low angle, creator crouched by the bag, straight to lens.
2. 0:03–0:09 — Insert: bag unzipped, damp tee pulled out, handheld.
3. 0:09–0:16 — MCU, tee held up, explaining the fiber point.
4. 0:16–0:23 — Product interaction: bottle from the floor, four sprays front and back, tee hung on a nail.
5. 0:23–0:27 — Wide, concrete, tee hanging, bottle on the floor in the foreground, sharp.
6. 0:27–0:30 — Back to creator, one line, cut on the word.

**On-screen text.** `0:01` *test it tomorrow, not now* · `0:12` *odor binds to synthetics* · `0:20` *no softeners = no stretch loss* · `0:28` *wear it again.*

**B-roll.** Bag zip macro · technical knit at fiber scale · tee hanging against concrete · bottle on concrete floor, hard shadow.

**CTA.** On-screen: `Get the Starter Kit — $34`. `AI-generated` label persistent.

## Casting and diversity note

The three creator personas above are written gender-neutral and age-ranged deliberately. Production should generate each concept across at least three visibly different creators — varying age, ethnicity, body type and voice — so that the paid tests measure *hook* performance rather than casting performance. Flexible per the Brand Lock; the script, the mechanism and the CTA stay fixed.
