The problem

You did everything right. It fell apart anyway.

You stopped buying five cheap sweaters a year and bought one good one. You read the fiber content. You paid more on purpose.

Four months later the shoulders are pilling, the cuffs have gone slack, and the thing you bought to last is already looking tired.

That's not age. That's forty minutes of wet fiber dragging against wet fiber, twice a week.[RTB-1]

The appliance you never questioned is the one taking the wardrobe apart.

The outcome

A wardrobe that still looks new in year three.

Not preserved in tissue paper. Worn — constantly — and still holding its shape, its color and its surface.

The change isn't buying better. You already did that. The change is what happens between wears.

The mechanism

Most of your laundry isn't dirty. It smells.

Two different problems. Dirt needs washing — nothing removes a stain but water and agitation, and AIRING won't pretend otherwise. Odor is a chemical compound sitting in the fiber, and it can be broken down where it is.

01

Enzymes break the compound down

The odor molecules that bind to wool and synthetic fiber are digested, not perfumed over.[RTB-2]

02

Molecular capture traps what's left

Remaining volatile compounds are held rather than released.[RTB-2]

03

Nothing stays behind

No softeners, no quaternary ammonium compounds, no residue that coats fiber and dulls it over time. Safe on merino, cashmere, raw denim and elastane.[RTB-3]

It doesn't replace washing. It replaces the washes you didn't need.

Objections

The questions worth asking

Isn't this just Febreze?

No — and you're right to ask, because most of this category is fragrance. Fragrance covers odor with a stronger smell. AIRING is enzyme-based: it breaks the odor compound down and captures what's left.[RTB-2] If you want the honest test, don't smell the garment while it's damp. Smell it tomorrow.

Isn't it unhygienic not to wash clothes?

Underwear, socks and anything against skin for a full day should be washed. Outer layers — knitwear, jeans, jackets, outer training layers — are a different case, and most of them go in the machine because of smell rather than soil. That's the load AIRING is for.

Will it damage wool or cashmere?

It's formulated around those fibers specifically: no softeners, no quats, no residue.[RTB-3] Patch-test a hem if you're unsure — with anything you apply to a garment you care about, you should.

Does it actually work?

We'd rather you find out than take our word for it, and we haven't got the customer history yet to point at. That's what the 30 days are for. Use it for a month. If your laundry pile hasn't shrunk, send it back.

What if I hate the smell?

Cold Air has no added fragrance at all.[RTB-6] Start there.
